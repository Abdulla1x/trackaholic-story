Trackaholic is a nutrition tracker. Describe a meal, say it out loud or snap a photo, and an AI estimate fills in the calories and macros, with weight trends, water, steps, supplements and a weekly review around it. I designed, planned and shipped it, growing it from a nine-line terminal script in 2024 into a live, installable web app with accounts and a phone layout.

How it started

July 2024

Serving size: one note a day.

15 July2024

By hand

When I started tracking in July 2024, it meant a kitchen scale, my phone's calculator and a notes app. I weighed the food, read the label, worked out weight divided by serving size times calories on the calculator, and typed the answer into that day's note. At the end of the day I added it up.

It worked, but every meal meant the same sums again, and a notes app can't tell you anything about last week.

16 July2024

One sum, automated

I was learning Python, so on 16 July 2024 I wrote a script that did the one sum I kept repeating. Ten days later it could add up a meal with several ingredients and save it under a date.

I used it every day that summer, and I still have the logs from those weeks.

On 24 July I wrote the script its first to-do list: saved foods with their macros, meals stored by date, edit and delete, macros looked up online, and catching bad input. Storing meals by date was done that same day, and most of the list is in the app today.

The redesign, before any code

Mid-September to early October 2026

Three weeks for a name, one for a whole new look.

About 2 weeksMid-Sep – 28 Sep

Finding what was wrong

Before designing anything, I spent about two weeks working out what was wrong. I went through every screen, asked people what they disliked about it, and wrote it all down. My conclusion was blunt: "In its current state I cannot onboard the app." So it would be a complete redesign, not a clean-up. The audit ended with 24 findings, led by six tabs, one card style for everything, a log page that opened on the manual form, and a stock slate-and-green look.

About 1 weekLate Sep – 3 Oct

Designing the redesign

Case study coming

I started with the layout. Three of the six tabs weren't really places: logging is an action, the weekly review is something you read once a week, and Weight mixed a daily weigh-in with a long-term trend. I placed every feature by how often I actually use it, tried four layouts as clickable wireframes, and chose three tabs, Today, Progress and You, with Log in the middle.

Then the look. Most trackers look alike, with white cards, rings and a healthy green, and mine did too. I compared four directions on the same screen, couldn't choose from still images, and picked the winner from a clickable trial: Fields, styled like a training computer's data page. The nutrition-label idea became an optional style.

The four directions as I compared them on 3 October, the same Today screen in each look.

About 3 weeks13 Sep – 2 Oct

Finding a name

"Macros Calculator" had become outdated. The app now does far more than count macros: AI meal estimates, weight trends, water, steps, supplements and a weekly review. The old name didn't do it justice. Finding a new one took about three weeks:

  1. Names about tracking and fitness. Almost all were taken.
  2. Two words combined. None of them felt right.
  3. Words from other languages, each with an obvious meaning, a hidden one, or both. Native speakers didn't like my shortlist.
  4. Names that just sound nice, without a meaning. I decided against that route.
  5. Talking it over with my brother, I dropped the rule that a name had to be completely unused, as long as nothing popular or related had it. Then "Trackaholic" came to me. It fit, because tracking is what the app is about now: meals, weight, water, steps and supplements, not only macros.

I bought the domain the same day, and the app took the name on 6 October. The "-aholic" could read as compulsive, so the tone stays playful.

RunningSince 5 Oct

Choosing the AI model

Results pending

The Gemini model the app relies on is being phased out, so I want to choose its replacement on evidence, not on a release note. I'm testing four newer models on the same reference meals, whose real values I know. The runs happen every day on a small home server, and I wrote down how the results would be scored before any came in. The write-up, with what I found, follows when the runs finish.

Where it's going

After the redesign

The tracking continues.

Training, next to nutrition

A workout tracker that's easy to pick up: log sets, reps and weights, and see your progressive overload over time. It lives in the same app, with today's workout beside today's meals and strength trends in Progress next to weight and food. It's the biggest addition planned after the redesign.

A front door

A landing page that opens instantly and a demo account full of data, so anyone can look around before signing up. Then real users beyond me, and later the app stores.

Fast from the first tap

No minute-long wake-up on the first visit, and screens that feel instant on a mid-range phone.

AI I can stand behind

Meal estimates close enough to trust, with the accuracy measured rather than assumed.

Good at the food I eat

South Asian and Gulf home cooking: salan, roti, khubz. The big national tables barely cover it, which makes it the place to be best.

It started as a sum I was tired of doing by hand. Each version since holds a little more of what I learned that year.
